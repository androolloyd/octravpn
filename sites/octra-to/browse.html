CIRCLES IN YOUR BROWSER

From oct://
to anywhere.

A public Circle is addressed by its ID and a path. Once a paid Circle route, DNS and TLS are active, any browser on the admitted Octranet can request it over ordinary HTTPS.

NATIVE CIRCLE LINK

oct://YOUR-CIRCLE/index.html

↓ HTTPS ON THE PAID ROUTE

https://circles.octra.to/circles/YOUR-CIRCLE/index.html

02 / REPLACE THE PREFIX

Keep the ID and path.

For another public Circle, replace oct:// with https://circles.octra.to/circles/ in the address bar. For example, oct://CIRCLE/docs/start.html becomes https://circles.octra.to/circles/CIRCLE/docs/start.html.

The paid gateway also accepts /resolve?u= followed by a percent-encoded native link and redirects to the same-origin HTTPS path. This is useful for apps that can construct links; DNS cannot do that translation by itself.

03 / VERIFY WHAT YOU SEE

Only public assets resolve.

The gateway reads the canonical Circle snapshot and pins subresources to that snapshot. Sealed or private assets do not become public because someone guessed their URL. A stale or unavailable node fails closed.

The URL pattern does not itself prove the paid gateway is active. Your operator must provide the route, DNS and a trusted HTTPS certificate before a browser can load it.

NO POPUP REQUIRED

Ordinary HTTPS.

When the route is deployed, the browser only sees an HTTPS address. It does not need an oct:// protocol handler or an Octra extension.

WHY A PAID ROUTE?

Someone carries the bytes.

This portal grants one bounded Circle read capability. An operator may charge for general Circle browsing and Internet traffic on the separate prepaid route.

Your path in.

The private devnet alpha is an invited developer test using the native Rust SDK. Start with a verified operator profile and a finite prepaid allowance. Custody and signing stay on your machine.

01 / GET THE INVITE

Accept the network you intend.

Your operator provides the chain, published tariff, pool and authenticated transport pins. The private profile uses a dedicated Linux client or VM. Open self-service signup and stock Tailscale private-payment onboarding are not available here.

02 / KEEP YOUR KEYS

Prepare your wallet locally.

Build the accepted Rust client and initialize its protected wallet and recovery journals. Generate your own credentials. An invitation provides accepted configuration and proving material; it never requires uploading your wallet seed, spend key or transaction signer.

03 / CHECK THE OFFER

Orient before you pay.

This Circle-hosted portal explains the network and funding path for free. Our published directory is oct4qzVq1RsPQnnoYpbXNuqjHYpPPs2LPdDK2inWZDwYCoP, offer 0, operated by oct1JBUrYbeTQazQ39LQn4ZjnPLHfDpLGXouqon1jZhJfYP.

The sealed default rate is 1,000 OU per MiB (0.001 devnet OCT/MiB). Verify the offer against canonical chain state. Your operator sets its prices; the protocol takes no commission.

04 / BUY FINITE TRAFFIC

Fund. Confirm. Connect.

Review a finite deposit and byte purchase in your local client. Forwarding requires actual successful native execution and canonical confirmation. A signed quote or HTTP success alone does not buy capacity. This alpha uses a test-setup shielded pool capped at one devnet OCT.

Available owned funds remain withdrawable. Settled purchases stay final. There are no automatic top-ups or operator credit lines.

05 / USE YOUR ROUTE

Test the connection you paid for.

The accepted launcher installs routing, DNS and physical-path guards. Confirm your exit IP and paid HTTPS traffic, then test restart with the original journal. Exhaustion closes the traffic path. The public deployment starts with one operator; separate operators are required for a public multi-hop route.
